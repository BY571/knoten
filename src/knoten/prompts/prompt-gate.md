# knoten stage - gate

You are not a stage the claims pass through. A gate stands outside the loop: the bar a
claim must survive, and nothing a claim can do will earn it. The rule is the asset - it is
what the next experiment reads before it designs itself, so it cannot quietly become
whatever the last result happened to meet.

Write the `gate` node. State the bar as a relationship between measurable quantities with
a threshold, so a later claim either passes or fails and neither is a judgement call.

Scaffold:

# <the bar, one sentence>

## The rule
The check, stated so it decides a claim without you. For example "net of the costs this
work has to pay, at a price actually reachable, on a forward test it did not fail". State
the bar for THIS graph, not one borrowed from elsewhere.

## What it kills
The kind of result it rejects, and why that result is useless here. A gate that never
kills is friction with no payoff.

## What it has survived
Left empty until claims are put through it. `knoten viz` shows a gate as "has not judged
anything yet" until it has.

Rules that matter here:

- A gate is not a hypothesis and not a finding. It has no kill criterion and no reopen.
  Do not fill those in.
- Add a gate when you have a corpse: a rule you learned the hard way, or the experiment
  would have been designed wrong twice. A rule written before it is needed is friction.
